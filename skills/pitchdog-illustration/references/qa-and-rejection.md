## Inspection setup

Never review only a chat thumbnail or generation receipt.

1. Open the candidate at actual-pixel size (100% of that file).
2. Open it again at roughly `10–15%` scale.
3. Put beside the approved-22 contact sheet.
4. Open two current finals and one Golden Six ancestor at actual-pixel size for
   the active profile; use canonical sources for exact-pixel adjudication.
5. Open exact subject evidence when likeness matters.
6. Hide the caption.

Inspect pixels first, then series context. A strong isolated image can still repeat another image or break the family.

## Gate sequence

Run gates in this order. Stop at a decisive failure; do not polish downstream details on a dead premise.

### 1. Rights and truth

- Inputs are authorized.
- Names, pronouns, relationships, and protected facts are correct.
- No private photograph, path, or metadata will leak into a public final.
- No third-party character, logo, artist imitation, or unlicensed reference became part of the output.

### 2. Two-second metaphor

- One familiar thing is immediately identifiable.
- One impossible behaviour is visibly happening.
- One ordinary act causes or meets that impossibility.
- Viewer can describe the event without caption.
- No arrow, label, symbol, or scenery is needed.

If this gate fails, kill or reconcept. Do not revise line quality.

### 3. Emotional truth

- The physical change reveals a specific kind of care, curiosity, nerve, attention, solidarity, humour, or practical intelligence.
- The subject's action belongs to them rather than a generic mascot.
- Wit is one brief semantic turn.
- Warmth comes from what the actor does, not a cute face.
- Image avoids domination, pity, greeting-card uplift, and fake profundity.

Ask: what material relationship changed? If answer is only “it feels magical”, the premise is thin.

### 4. Identity

- Subject reads as the exact person or animal at thumbnail size.
- Decisive cues survive at actual-pixel size.
- Body, age, skin tone, hair or coat, markings, clothing, and proportions remain truthful.
- Pose and gaze support the action.
- No beautifying, thinning, gender drift, breed substitution, or stock-character default appeared.
- Likeness detail has not pushed image into realism.

If identity is generic but the visual system is correct, regenerate from stronger labelled identity evidence. Do not add fur or facial rendering indiscriminately.

### 5. Visual law

- Field is full bleed, cool or neutral pale pastel, and visibly not a white stage.
- No yellow, amber, sepia, mustard, cream, or dirty-beige cast.
- Motif remains small and lower-field.
- Roughly 80% calm negative space remains.
- Near-black line is sparse and slightly imperfect.
- One signal accent has one semantic job.
- Grain is quiet.
- No glossy vector, 3D, watercolour, cinematic light, vignette, glow, or shadow theatre.
- No scenery, filler, extra props, marks, text, border, or watermark.

### 6. Physical clarity

- Hand, paw, mouth, gaze, and object contact make sense.
- Weight, balance, suspension, fold, gap, line, or other changed physics is visible.
- No extra limbs, merged forms, floating contact, broken anatomy, accidental face, or impossible action unrelated to the concept.
- Accent object connects to the intended actor and object.

For every animal, write a literal anatomy ledger at actual-pixel size: count each
visible leg and paw, trace it to the body, and account for occluded limbs. Repeat
after any edit and in the final contact sheet. Do not approve by gestalt.

### 7. Composition

- One remembered silhouette survives blur and thumbnail tests.
- Visual mass does not drift to centre or fill the canvas.
- Subject and impossible object have a clear relation.
- No accidental tangent, crop, edge collision, or empty-space dead zone distracts.
- Field feels active, not vacant.

### 8. Technical final

- Dimensions and aspect ratio match destination.
- Image is opaque, clean, and correctly oriented.
- Colour profile and export do not introduce a cast.
- Filename, status, metadata, alt text, and checksum are correct.
- Candidate and source remain preserved.

## Decision rule

### Promote

Promote only when every gate passes and no higher-consequence weakness remains.

### Revise once

Make one surgical revision when:

- metaphor is alive;
- identity is mostly right;
- composition and visual law mostly hold;
- one local failure can be named precisely;
- change can be written as `change only X` with invariants.

Examples: one wrong shoe, one scale mismatch, stray mark, accent misconnection, slight field cast.

### Kill and reconcept

Kill when:

- caption performs the idea;
- familiar object or changed physics is unclear;
- subject is a portrait beside the metaphor;
- multiple systems are wrong;
- identity is generic;
- image needs scenery or symbols;
- work is polished but emotionally vacant;
- revision would preserve a dead premise;
- same failure survives two attempts;
- only sunk cost argues for continuing.

Record one useful failure reason. Do not write a defence of the candidate.

## Group and series checks

### Group

- Relative scale matches lock.
- Every subject has one visible point of agency.
- Centrality, size, darkness, accent, and prop ownership do not create an unintended hero.
- Each identity survives independently.
- Shared task reads as shared.

### Series

- No repeated object, verb, physical turn, silhouette, field sequence, or caption mechanism makes the set formulaic.
- Same subject stays visually consistent.
- Different subjects remain distinct.
- Current authority is coherent without becoming a template.
- Contact sheet has rhythm: calm variation, no one accidental outlier.
- No image exists only to fill a quota.

## Revision discipline

- Preserve original candidate.
- Write diagnosis before edit prompt.
- Change one thing.
- Restate all invariants.
- Compare revised image with original and authorities.
- Do not call a revision better because it is newer.
- If repair introduces a new higher-cost failure, reject it.

## Review output

Use [candidate review template](../templates/candidate-review.md).

Lead with one verdict:

- `PROMOTE — all gates pass`
- `REVISE ONCE — core alive; bounded failure: ...`
- `KILL — premise fails because ...`

Then record concrete evidence, not taste adjectives. Never assign a numerical soul, warmth, poetry, or wit score.
