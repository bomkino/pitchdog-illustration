## Evidence hierarchy

Use identity evidence in this order:

1. latest explicit owner correction or current written identity lock;
2. several recent, clear source photographs from useful angles;
3. current locked illustration of the same subject;
4. current approved relationship or scale law;
5. older approved or contextual evidence;
6. inference, labelled as inference.

Never infer a permanent trait from one expression, pose, outfit, or photograph. Never use source-photo scenery, lighting, camera distortion, or realism as style authority.

If identity-critical evidence is absent, complete concept work and production setup, then name the exact missing evidence. Do not invent likeness.

## Character lock

Before generating, record only decisive cues:

- name and pronouns;
- silhouette at thumbnail scale;
- face or muzzle geometry;
- hair, ears, tail, or coat geometry;
- body proportions and relative scale;
- skin, coat, and marking colours;
- posture and habitual action language;
- clothing or accessories that are protected;
- cues that models repeatedly get wrong;
- prohibited substitutions or stereotypes.

Use [character lock template](../templates/character-lock.md).

Keep this lock verbatim across revisions. Restate it on every edit.

## Humane simplification

Editorial economy removes detail, not dignity.

- Preserve body shape instead of normalising it.
- Preserve age instead of youth-washing.
- Preserve skin tone instead of lightening or homogenising it.
- Preserve gender expression instead of applying model defaults.
- Preserve mobility aids, scars, cultural clothing, hair texture, and other meaningful cues when present.
- Do not beautify, thin, masculinise, feminise, infantilise, or glamorise without explicit authority.
- Avoid caricaturing features linked to race, gender, age, disability, class, or body size.

Use posture, silhouette, hair or coat geometry, and one small expression before adding facial detail. Likeness should survive simplification; realism is not the solution.

## Human identity

- Keep full body visible when body language carries likeness.
- Name feet, ankles, trousers, shoes, hands, and object contact when generations repeatedly drift there.
- Describe clothing by cut, fit, and function, not gender stereotype alone.
- Keep hand actions simple and physically plausible.
- Let expression remain private and task-directed; avoid generic smiling at camera.
- Compare at thumbnail scale for silhouette and at full size for decisive cues.

When one area is wrong but the concept and rest of identity are alive, edit only that area and restate all invariants. If face, body, posture, clothing, and metaphor all drift, regenerate.

## Animal identity

Breed labels are weak evidence. Record:

- overall size and body length;
- head and muzzle shape;
- ear set, fold, feathering, and tip colour;
- coat colour and exact marking geometry;
- tail shape and carriage;
- chest, blaze, socks, brows, and eye patches;
- lean, stocky, long, compact, or other proportion cues;
- characteristic stance and attention.

Keep fur flat and economical. Do not solve identity with photorealistic texture.

At actual-pixel size for the active profile, run an anatomy ledger after every
generation or edit:

1. enumerate every visible leg and paw;
2. trace each visible limb back to its shoulder or hip;
3. account explicitly for every occluded limb;
4. separate overlapping paws from duplicated anatomy;
5. repeat the count in the final contact sheet.

“Looks plausible” is not a count. Reject any extra, merged, detached, or
unaccounted limb even when the metaphor and face are strong.

## Group dignity

For every group image:

- state relative scale numerically when known;
- compare shoulder or head heights in the same plane;
- give each subject a visible action or equal share of one action;
- prevent central placement, size, colour, or prop ownership from creating an unintended hero;
- preserve each identity independently;
- check pronouns and relationships;
- reject mascot/sidekick hierarchy.

Equal love is not identical pose. Distinct character can coexist with equal narrative dignity.

## Identity QA

Ask:

- Would an owner recognise the subject with caption hidden?
- Does silhouette match at thumbnail size?
- Do scale, body, hair or coat, markings, clothing, and posture survive at full size?
- Did the model add a breed, body, beauty, age, gender, or race default?
- Did likeness detail make the image realistic, glossy, or crowded?
- In a group, can each subject be named and does each retain equal dignity?

Reject a generic approximation even when it is attractive.
