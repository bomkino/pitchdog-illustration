## Target

Create a tiny editorial visual poem: witty, warm, handmade, immediately legible, and emotionally true.

The image should feel found, not manufactured. Restraint must carry the authorship. Do not confuse fewer objects with simplicity; one weak symbol on an empty field is still weak.

## Canvas and composition

- Use the actual destination dimensions whenever known. If no placement exists,
  default to portrait `3:4` at `1536 × 2048 px`, matching the dominant current
  family format. Treat model-native output sizes as working renders; crop or
  resize only after protecting the metaphor and negative space.
- Fill the complete canvas with one pale field. Never place the illustration on a white card, page, vignette, spotlight, or floating stage.
- Keep the meaningful motif in the lower or lower-middle field.
- Reserve roughly 80% of the canvas as active calm. Multi-subject work may use slightly more space only when every body remains small and the field still dominates.
- Build one silhouette readable at thumbnail size.
- Keep the horizon absent unless the impossible relationship literally requires one spare line.
- Use no environment beyond the minimum physical line needed for the metaphor.
- Let edge relationships, scale, contact, and direction carry movement. Do not add motion marks by habit.

### Starting field anchors

Use these as cool starting points, not a mandatory rotation:

| Field | sRGB start | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Powder | `#E4ECF5` | cool, clear, open |
| Lilac | `#F2EBF8` | reflective, tender |
| Mint | `#EAF1E8` | practical, quiet |
| Blush | `#FDE9EE` | warm without beige |
| Neutral | `#F6F5F8` | use carefully; must not read as white stage |

Adjust for the destination and approved reference. Preserve a cool or neutral paper impression. Reject any amber, cream, sepia, mustard, dirty-beige, or yellow AI cast unless an explicit owner-approved exception changes the law.

## Field, ink, material, and accent

### Field

- Use one full-bleed colour.
- Keep value variation extremely low.
- Avoid gradients that advertise themselves, edge darkening, bloom, haze, and atmospheric light.

### Ink

- Use near-black, not polished pure vector black. Start near `#0E0E0F`.
- Keep line sparse, slightly uneven, and pressure-aware.
- Let filled near-black shapes appear only where they help the metaphor or decisive silhouette.
- Avoid uniform Bézier smoothness, perfect geometric corners, glossy contour, airbrush shading, and faux-pencil noise on every edge.

### Material

- Do not add grain, noise, paper fibres, or distressed overlays to new work.
- Carry the handmade quality through pressure-aware ink, scale, contact, and
  the physical relationship, not a texture layer.
- Preserve texture intrinsic to approved source art without amplifying it.
  Older reference texture is not a requirement for a new image.
- Do not denoise or otherwise alter approved originals without edit authority.

### Accent

- Use one restrained signal colour for the conceptual turn. Coral/vermilion near `#F34325` is the house default; cobalt may serve a different semantic job.
- Let the accent mark the handle, thread, mat, rung, bridge, opening, carried object, or other decisive relation.
- Do not distribute accent evenly as decoration.
- Truthful skin, hair, coat, clothing, and identity colours do not count as decorative accent, but keep them controlled.

## Character and object rendering

- Start from a tiny editorial glyph, not a portrait.
- Preserve decisive silhouette, posture, hair or coat geometry, proportions, and one or two identity cues.
- Use flat, modest colour with minimal modelling.
- Keep expressions small and private; avoid camera-facing mascot smiles.
- Make the ordinary action physically clear: mouth holds, paw touches, hand folds, body leans, gaze attends.
- Keep the impossible object simpler than the character.
- Draw objects as familiar enough to identify in two seconds, then change one physical rule.
- Avoid photorealistic fur, skin, fabric, reflections, depth of field, rim light, cast-shadow theatre, and volumetric form.

## Visual physics

Each image contains one controlled impossibility:

1. One familiar thing keeps its basic identity.
2. One physical rule changes.
3. The subject performs an ordinary act that makes the changed rule tender, useful, brave, curious, or funny.
4. The relationship remains visible without arrows, text, or explanatory scenery.

Strong impossibilities change weight, obedience, material, timing, containment, scale, continuity, or direction. Weak impossibilities add random surreal props.

Only one changed rule per image. A knot that walks, talks, glows, and becomes a door is four jokes and no poem.

## Hard exclusions

Reject immediately:

- white stage, floating card, frame, spotlight, or isolated sticker treatment;
- yellow, amber, sepia, mustard, cream, or dirty-beige cast;
- polished vector line, clip-art geometry, glossy editorial finish, 3D, watercolour wash, cinematic light, or realistic portrait rendering;
- large central character or object that turns negative space into margin;
- scenery, rooms, landscapes, props without jobs, decorative dots, stars, leaves, swashes, or confetti;
- multiple jokes, explanatory arrows, labels, text, icons, or symbol soup;
- generic dog, stock human, corporate mascot, greeting-card sentiment, children's-book sweetness, or cute pose without specific attention;
- caption-dependent meaning;
- copied approved composition with nouns replaced;
- technically neat but emotionally vacant work.

## Final visual checks

- Blur or zoom out: one silhouette remains.
- View at `10–15%`: relationship reads in two seconds.
- View at `100%`: identity cues and line imperfection survive; no stray artefacts.
- Convert mentally to grayscale: relationship and hierarchy remain.
- Remove accent mentally: image still makes sense; accent deepens rather than rescues it.
- Check material against the source: no added or amplified texture; approved
  source art remains intact.
- Cover the caption: metaphor remains visible.
- Compare against current approved contact sheet: same family, new thought.
